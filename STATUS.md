## Angular mean — revised frontier

`notes/mean_block_zero_force_audit.md` identified the angular Fourier mode `0` as the remaining local zero-force problem. Section 8 of the OpenAI construction contains radial pressure/stress inversion, a torus zero-average correction, and an explicit five-dimensional moment correction.

Two new results now sharpen this frontier.

### 1. Fast-time torus divisor is removed in the forward formulation

`proofs/characteristic_fast_time_mean_inverse.md` proves that the source cohomological inverse along `v_t\cdot\partial_y` is **not a fundamental obstruction** for the preferred local input-output architecture.

Instead of freezing slow time `T`, solve along the actual normalized physical-time characteristics of

\[
\boxed{t_*=-\varepsilon\partial_T+c_iN_i.}
\]

On a fixed normalized fast-time strip the exact causal inverse is

\[
(\mathcal J_iF)(T,y)
=-\frac1\varepsilon
\int_T^{T_0}
F\!\left(\tau,y+\frac{c_i}{\varepsilon}(T-\tau)v_t\right)d\tau.
\]

It satisfies

\[
\boxed{t_*\mathcal J_iF=F}
\]

with one entrance/exit value prescribed, and in every translation-invariant torus norm obeys

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal J_iF\|\le L\|F\|
}
\]

without any Diophantine denominator or torus derivative loss.

Thus a Nash--Moser iteration is **not needed merely to invert the fast-time mean equation**.

### 2. Radial compactification remainder has a quantitative Gevrey tail

`proofs/gevrey_radial_compactification_remainder.md` treats the remaining radial compactification operator `A_1`. Assuming the fixed-loss/positive-gain estimate supplied by the source finite-stage calculus,

\[
\|A_1f\|_{C^m}
\le C^{m+1}\varepsilon^\delta\|f\|_{C^{m+r}},
\]

one obtains for compactly supported Gevrey-`s>1` data a radius-losing estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|A_1f\|_{G^s_{L'}}
\le C\varepsilon^\delta\|f\|_{G^s_L}.
}
\]

Optimizing a finite number `N(epsilon)` of iterations yields the explicit stretched-exponential tail

\[
\boxed{
\|A_1^Nf\|_{G^s_{L_\infty}}
\le
\exp(-c\varepsilon^{-\mu})\|f\|_{G^s_L}
}
\]

while preserving a positive limiting Gevrey radius.

This is stronger than an uncontrolled all-orders `C^infty` flatness statement and is suitable as an input to a final exact forward fixed-point problem.
