# Principal two-event amplitude fixed ray

**Status:** PROVED ALGEBRAIC RENEWAL THEOREM, CONDITIONAL ONLY ON TWO NONZERO NORMALIZED DESIGNATED COEFFICIENTS. The exact source normalization of those coefficients across the two collars must still be audited before this becomes a source-embedded theorem.

This note solves the finite-dimensional problem isolated in `two_collar_zero_residual_reduction.md` at the algebraic principal level.

## 1. Two designated quadratic coefficients

Let

\[
A_2\in\mathbb C,
\qquad
A_1\in\mathbb C
\]

be the incoming complex amplitudes of the beta-two parent and unit-beta catalyst. Reality of the physical field is enforced by adding conjugate packets.

The first designated difference interaction has the principal form

\[
\boxed{
D_1=\kappa_1 A_2\overline{A_1},
}
\tag{AF1}
\]

where `kappa_1!=0` is the normalized projection/transport coefficient for

\[
2-1\to1.
\]

The second designated sum interaction uses the surviving catalyst and the new child:

\[
\boxed{
R_2=\kappa_2 A_1D_1,
}
\tag{AF2}
\]

where `kappa_2!=0` is the normalized coefficient for

\[
1+1\to2.
\]

The principal two-event renewal map is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R_0(A_2,A_1)
=\left(
\kappa_1\kappa_2 A_2|A_1|^2,
\kappa_1A_2\overline{A_1}
\right).
}
\tag{AF3}
\]

Nonvanishing of the two geometric polarization factors is already proved in `v08_designated_projection_nonvanishing.md` and `v08_beta2_feedback_renewal.md`. What is not yet audited here is the complete source-normalized value of `kappa_1,kappa_2` after all envelope/time-integration factors are included.

## 2. Fixed-ray solutions exist for every nonzero coefficient pair

We seek

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R_0(A_2,A_1)=\lambda(A_2,A_1)
}
\tag{AF4}

with

\[
A_1A_2\lambda\ne0.
\]

From the first component of (AF3),

\[
\boxed{
\lambda=\kappa_1\kappa_2|A_1|^2.
}
\tag{AF5}

The second component requires

\[
\kappa_1A_2\overline{A_1}
=\kappa_1\kappa_2|A_1|^2A_1.
\]

Canceling `kappa_1` and writing

\[
A_1=re^{i\phi},\qquad r>0,
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
A_2=\kappa_2 r^2e^{2i\phi}.
}
\tag{AF6}

Thus for every `r>0` and every phase `phi`,

\[
\boxed{
A_*(r,\phi)
=\left(
\kappa_2r^2e^{2i\phi},
re^{i\phi}
\right)
}
\tag{AF7}

is a nonzero fixed ray, with multiplier

\[
\boxed{
\lambda(r)=\kappa_1\kappa_2r^2.
}
\tag{AF8}

Therefore the principal two-event map has a two-real-parameter family of nonzero renewal rays whenever

\[
\boxed{\kappa_1\kappa_2\ne0.}
\tag{AF9}

No additional algebraic resonance condition is needed.

## 3. Unit-modulus renewal can always be normalized

Choose

\[
\boxed{
r_*:=|\kappa_1\kappa_2|^{-1/2}.}
\tag{AF10}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{|\lambda(r_*)|=1.}
\tag{AF11}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
|A_{1,*}|=|\kappa_1\kappa_2|^{-1/2},
\qquad
|A_{2,*}|=\frac1{|\kappa_1|}.
}
\tag{AF12}
\]

Thus the principal renewal cell never has an unavoidable multiplicative amplitude loss: its input amplitudes can be normalized so that the common fixed-ray multiplier has unit modulus.

This does **not** mean the multiplier is exactly `+1`; its phase is

\[
\arg\lambda=\arg(\kappa_1\kappa_2).
\tag{AF13}
\]

## 4. Phase reset is available from the two-generator torus gauge

The nonzero lattice is generated by two independent characters. A translation of the auxiliary torus center by `c in T^2` sends generator amplitudes to

\[
A_j\mapsto e^{2\pi i e_j\cdot c}A_j.
\tag{AF14}
\]

Because `e_1,e_2` are an integer basis of the two-generator character lattice, the two phases

\[
e^{2\pi ie_1\cdot c},\qquad e^{2\pi ie_2\cdot c}
\]

may be chosen independently.

Therefore the phase accumulated by a unit-modulus multiplier does not constitute an algebraic recurrence obstruction: at the next sparse supernode one may choose the torus translation so that both incoming generator phases are reset to the reference values while leaving their magnitudes unchanged.

This is a gauge/placement operation already present in the translated-supernode architecture; it does not alter physical carrier magnitude or beta.

## 5. Persistence under a small exact PDE perturbation

The exact two-collar reduction has

\[
\mathscr R_\ell=\mathscr R_0+\mathcal E_\ell,
\qquad
\|\mathcal E_\ell\|_{C^1(K)}=o(1)
\tag{AF15}
\]

on fixed compact amplitude sets, once the source-normalized coefficient statement is verified.

For magnitudes define

\[
F_0(x,y)
=\left(
|\kappa_1\kappa_2|xy^2-x,
|\kappa_1|xy-y
\right).
\tag{AF16}
\]

At the unit-modulus fixed magnitude

\[
x_*=|\kappa_1|^{-1},
\qquad
y_*=|\kappa_1\kappa_2|^{-1/2},
\]

the Jacobian is

\[
D F_0(x_*,y_*)
=\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 2|\kappa_1\kappa_2|x_*y_*\\
|\kappa_1|y_* & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{AF17}
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\boxed{
\det D F_0(x_*,y_*)
=-2|\kappa_1|^2|\kappa_2|x_*y_*^2\ne0.
}
\tag{AF18}

Hence the magnitude fixed point is transverse. By the implicit-function theorem, every sufficiently small `C^1` perturbation of the magnitude renewal map has a nearby nonzero fixed magnitude pair.

The two independent torus phases then supply the phase-reset coordinates.

Accordingly, once (AF15) is established in the exact source normalization, the principal fixed ray is **structurally stable** under the small zero-residual correction.
