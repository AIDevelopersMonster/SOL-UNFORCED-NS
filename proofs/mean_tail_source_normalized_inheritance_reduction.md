# Mean-tail inheritance in source-normalized local jets

**Status:** PROVED DISCRETE/SCALE REDUCTION; ONE SOURCE-SPECIFIC OSEEN JET-PROPAGATION ESTIMATE REMAINS OPEN. This note attacks the global obstruction explicitly left open by `coupled_phase_adapted_local_zero_residual_theorem.md`: the whole-space angular-mean/pressure tail produced by earlier exact relay cells must not destroy the source-normalized geometry of later cells.

The key point is that the next relay does **not** require the accumulated mean tail to be absolutely summable in its unscaled whole-space `H^{m_0}` norm. It requires the tail and finitely many of its physical derivatives to be small after multiplication by the shrinking source length/velocity scales on the later relay core.

The variable-step schedule of `native_variable_step_exact_cross_scale_tiling.md` makes this quantitative. Under one natural one-stage Oseen jet estimate, the inherited mean may grow like a tiny negative power `q^{-mu}` and still disappear in every source-normalized local jet because `mu` can be chosen far below the geometric exponents `D=1/2-h` and `1/2`.

## 1. Data already proved locally

Let `m_j` denote the angular-mean correction generated by exact stage `j`. The local theorem gives, for fixed `m_0>=6`,

\[
\boxed{
\|m_j\|_{\mathfrak M_{\sigma,m_0}}
\le C S_j^{C_0}\varepsilon_j^{1-\kappa_s},
\qquad
\kappa_s=10^{-5},
}
\tag{MT1}

where

\[
\varepsilon_j=q_j^h,
\qquad
0<h\le10^{-3}.
\tag{MT2}

The same local fixed-point theorem has mean Lipschitz factors carrying positive epsilon powers and an exact whole-space Leray--Oseen propagator on one relay collar.

## 2. Variable-step scale schedule

Use

\[
\boxed{
q_{j+1}=q_j(1-\delta_j),
\qquad
\delta_j=\Lambda\varepsilon_jS_j,
}
\tag{MT3}

with fixed `Lambda>0` and sufficiently late starting level so that `0<delta_j<1/2`.

Then

\[
-\log(1-\delta_j)
\asymp\delta_j
\tag{MT4}

uniformly, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{q_j}{q_{j+1}}
\asymp
\Lambda\varepsilon_jS_j.
}
\tag{MT5}

This identity converts small per-stage losses into powers of the physical scale `q`.

## 3. Correct inherited quantity

Let

\[

For a later source core of scale `q_j`, the geometric background normalizations are

\[
A=\frac12+h,

The quantities that perturb the normalized local relay coefficients are of the form

\[

\[

Because `m_0>=6`, Sobolev embedding converts (MT8) into bounded physical finite jets with the same power `q_j^{-mu}`.

## 4. One-stage estimate needed from the Oseen block

The missing source-specific statement is the following.

### Oseen inheritance hypothesis `OH(mu)`

There are constants `C_O,C_F,C_1` independent of sufficiently high `j` such that the cumulative mean norm satisfies

\[
\boxed{
N_{j+1}
\le
\exp(C_O\varepsilon_jS_j)N_j
+
C_F S_j^{C_1}\varepsilon_j^{1-\kappa_s},
}
\tag{MT9}

where `N_j` controls the finite physical/phase-adapted jets needed in (MT6)--(MT7).

The additive term is exactly the local forcing scale (MT1). The crucial content is the **near-identity homogeneous factor** `exp(C_O epsilon_j S_j)` over one native variable step, rather than a fixed factor bigger than one.

This is the only analytic estimate not proved in this note.

## 5. Homogeneous amplification becomes a tiny q-power

Iterating only the homogeneous factor in (MT9),

\[
\prod_{k<j}
\exp(C_O\varepsilon_kS_k)
=
\exp\left(C_O\sum_{k<j}\varepsilon_kS_k\right).
\]

By (MT5),

\[
\sum_{k<j}\varepsilon_kS_k
\le
\frac{C}{\Lambda}
\log\frac{q_0}{q_j}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{k<j}
\exp(C_O\varepsilon_kS_k)
\le
C q_j^{-C'_O/\Lambda}.
}
\tag{MT10}

Hence increasing the fixed design parameter `Lambda` makes the inherited homogeneous growth exponent arbitrarily small.

## 6. Additive forcing produces only the intrinsic tiny exponent `h kappa_s`

Fix any `eta>0`. Because `S_j` is polynomial in the native/source band index whereas `q_j->0`, after increasing the starting level we may absorb every fixed polynomial power into an arbitrarily small q-loss:

\[
\boxed{

}
\tag{MT11}

Let

\[
\mu>h\kappa_s+\eta+\frac{C'_O}{\Lambda}.
\tag{MT12}

For small `delta_j`, the scale step gives

\[
q_{j+1}^{-\mu}-q_j^{-\mu}
\ge
c\mu\delta_j q_j^{-\mu}

\tag{MT13}

The forcing in (MT9) obeys, using (MT11),

\[

\tag{MT14}

Condition (MT12) makes the exponent in (MT14) no worse than the comparison increment (MT13), after increasing the starting level and constants.

A discrete comparison/induction therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
N_j\le C_\mu q_j^{-\mu}.
}
\tag{MT15}

The irreducible forcing exponent is only

\[
\boxed{h\kappa_s\le10^{-8}.}
\tag{MT16}

The additional homogeneous exponent `C'_O/Lambda` can be made as small as desired by selecting `Lambda` large and then starting sufficiently late so that `Lambda epsilon_j S_j<1/2`.

## 7. Source-normalized inherited mean vanishes

Choose `eta` small and `Lambda` large enough that

\[
\boxed{
\mu<D=\frac12-h.
}
\tag{MT17}

This is an enormous margin because `D>=0.499` while the intrinsic exponent in (MT16) is at most `10^{-8}`.

\[
\boxed{

\tag{MT18}

\[
\boxed{

\boxed{

The same argument applies to every fixed derivative word whose source normalization carries a positive power strictly larger than `mu`.

Thus, conditional on (MT9), the entire inherited whole-space mean tail becomes an `o(1)` perturbation of the frozen normalized relay coefficients and cannot close a previously strict action/polarization/source-cone margin.
