\[
\boxed{M:=2D-P=D-C.}

After old `C` has been routed out of the second collision, the leading parents are

\[

\qquad

\[
\boxed{C_{new}:=P-D,}
\tag{MR1}

\[

\[
\boxed{M:=D-C_{new}=2D-P.}
\tag{MR2}

\[
\boxed{\beta(M)=0.}
\tag{MR3}

\[
M=D-C.
\tag{MR4}

Thus `M` is the zero-tangential / beta-zero root of the dangerous sideband family.

## 6. `M` is a mean/zero-tangential mode, but not automatically zero

Equation (MR3) places `M` in the beta-zero / zero-tangential sector. In the phase-adapted coefficient formulation this sector belongs to the forward mean/viscous block rather than to a positive-beta pulse envelope.

However the exact local mean theorem solves

\[
m=\mathcal T_\ell(m)
\]

for the mean required to make the physical residual vanish. It does **not** impose `m=0`, nor does it impose the particular coefficient `M=0`.

Therefore one must not conclude from `beta(M)=0` that the dangerous family disappears automatically.

## 7. Nonvanishing of the root source

The root is generated by a difference interaction between two unit-beta positive-slope growing packets, `D` and `C_new`. For equal positive betas, the beta-zero target normal has no tangential component. The principal difference vector is

\[
B_-
=(s_D-s_C)(g(s_D)+g(s_C)).
\tag{MR24}
\]

Projecting orthogonally to the beta-zero radial target removes only the radial component. The remaining tangential/normal components are proportional to

\[
-(s_D+s_C)K
+c_0\left(\sqrt{1+s_D^2}+\sqrt{1+s_C^2}\right)N.
\tag{MR25}
\]

For positive slopes neither coefficient vanishes. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_M B_-\ne0.
}
\tag{MR26}
\]

Thus the mean root is not removed by a principal Leray/polarization zero.

## 8. Why an endpoint mean parameter is not yet enough

The global fixed-order mean propagator gives a legitimate causal entrance-state evolution. One may therefore consider an entrance parameter

\[
m(s_-;\mu)=m_0+\mu\phi_M
\]

in the `M` direction. The exact mean fixed point will depend `C^1` on `mu`, and the linearized exit derivative is the nonzero forward propagation of `phi_M` plus source-small feedback.

This can control an **exit functional** `M_out` under a standard transversality hypothesis.

But the dangerous modes (MR13)--(MR22) are generated while `M` is present inside the collision interval. Setting only

\[
M_{out}=0
\]

does not undo sidebands already created earlier. Therefore scalar endpoint control does not by itself close the gate.

A valid root-control architecture must suppress the `M` trajectory on the interaction region, or at least annihilate the finite collection of sideband output moments generated from it.
